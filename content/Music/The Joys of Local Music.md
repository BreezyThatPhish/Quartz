For the longest time I was never on any kind of music streaming service, I was a nerd and used youtube for everything. Then, a few years ago, I moved to Spotify because my brother had a spare slot in his family plan. 
99% of my music was a local collection, and after a couple months I figured it was pretty stupid to be using a streaming service for a local collection, so I looked into alternatives, and thats how I found bandcamp and the world of local music collections. 

The entire thing is a tradeoff with pros and cons, but one I believe to be firmly worth it should you be in the position to accommodate for it (though everyone is different of course). 
Instead of paying for a subscription, you pay per piece of music; more expensive in the short term, but an incredible amount saved in the long term. And instead of having it all in cloud storage, you keep it all locally, though with the ability to download it from any device; demands good data management or decent storage space, but it means it can never be taken away from you should you stop paying a subscription or if the service goes down. 
There is more responsibility on you as a consumer, but you have so much more control thanks to it.

Additionally, you typically end up supporting artists better than you would be via streaming services, as Im sure it doesnt need to be said that theyre renown for paying jack squat. As part of this, a lot of artists will include extra goodies as part of this more acute support. More for them, more for you.

Ive found a lot of joy in the softwares that focus on local collections, theyre almost always far more dynamic, versatile and customisable. 
The most popular one from my experience is foobar, though I gravitate more towards Strawberry.
![[Pasted image 20261109212722.png]]
I find its UI incredibly comfortable and intuitive, I love to use it. Disclaimer that whilst its free on linux, its paid for on windows due to the dev finding it more of a chore to maintain that side of things. Even if not strawberry, there are dozens of options out there, youre bound to find one that you like, one that might even change the way you interface with your music entirely.

Another thing that comes with local music in my experience is a bridge into a wonderful little pocket of the hardware world being DAP's (digital audio players). There are lots of solutions to carry local music around on your phone, but I really enjoy having a dedicated device for it, and there are tons out there. 
The one I have currently is a Hifi Walker H2, a serious brick of a device; a solid rectangle of all buttons and no touchscreen. Admittedly I dont like the UI interface too much, but I couldnt be happier with the physicality of the thing, and besides I can always get around to jailbreaking it and putting rockbox on there for some greater customisation.
