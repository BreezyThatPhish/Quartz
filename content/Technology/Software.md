#### <font style="color:grey"> ilo ko </font>

I've spent the last few years really discovering and learning various softwares that best fit my mould, and theres always more out there to find. Software can be anything from a new tool that completely changes how you conduct your work to a new toy to play with, its incredibly fun to explore. 

In that time, Ive grown favourites, of which are listed below. 

<br>

**EndeavourOS**
My choice of OS, an arch based distribution thats wrapped up in a very user friendly but robust package. Its been a rock solid home for me a few years now, and is my goto distro recommendation. 
I choose linux for a variety of reasons, but the predominant one is the control and ownership of it. The ability to tinker, tweak and change my OS for practical or utterly silly reasons is very very compelling to me, and that true sense of control is what keeps me on this side of the fence. 
Your OS should enable you to do the things you want to do with as much ability and as little friction as possible, for me linux is that OS, but its a highly personal decision, for which there is no right answer. The only thing I can encourage is experimentation. 

**KDE**
I choose KDE as my desktop environment mostly for the usability and flexibility of it. Its not the most minimalist by any means, but I get good use out of a lot of its functionalities, and it gives me a lot of things to play with. 

**Krohnkite** 
On the topic of OS tinkering, Krohnkite has been something I've grown to love over the past year or so, it being a community script that brings tiling window functionality to KDE. 
I've had multiple stints trying dedicated twm's like hyprland or Sway, but I always find myself lamenting the stability that the fleshed out suite of a prepackaged desktop environment brings. Krohnkite bridges that gap, by allowing me to utilise twm abilities ontop of a comfortable bedrock of existing tools. 

A variety of preset layouts, easy access to more traditional window floating, largely impermanent install-wise and with a nice array of rules & settings to tweak your experience, its been a comfortable home for a while and likely will remain so for the foreseeable future.

**Obsidian**
Its impossible for me to overstate how revolutionary Obsidian has been in my day to day life. A fantastically robust note taking app thats strength lies in its underlying simplicity, that then allows you to to build as simple or as complicated of a system within it as you want; its like a sandbox. 
All your notes are lightweight and local markdown text files with no need to access any cloud nonsense, and with a huge expanse of community plugins it feels like there are few things you cant make it do. 

My bread and butter are tasks, bases and dashboards. 
The tasks plugin allows me to setup and schedule a variety of reminders that do great for keeping me organised and on track (its the only reason my plants are alive given I use it for my watering schedule).

Bases allow you to create big lists of files that follow a variety of rules, and given my thorough use of file properties across all my notes, I can get real precise with what I want to list and where. 
I then take these two things, and through the use of [[MCL Multi Column.css]] to make those callout grids, arrange them into these big dashboards that I make for any large topic I want to make record of.

Alongside this organisation ability it must be stated how fundamentally brilliant Obsidians philosophy is. 
It aims to be a personal wiki, allowing you to link files together, and encouraging you to keep information decentralised and connected. This way, relevant information naturally remains together, and things being kept more individual means they are easier to regularly re-use.

A good example of how this is useful has always been my university work. I've been taking notes in obsidian since my first day (it was actually the reason I found the app to begin with, looking up Linux-friendly note taking apps), and so I have lots and lots of information from my study tenure kept around. 
Theory of mind was a major topic in one of my earlier modules, and at the time I wrote a dedicated note about it, as well as adding to it continually as more information came up. 
Then, throughout the subsequent years of work, whenever I referenced Theory of Mind anywhere else I just linked to that note, no need to re-write everything. 
Equally, if I want to now find material relating to Theory of Mind, I can just look at what notes link to it.
(Highlighted lines are notes that link directly to Theory of Mind, unhighlighted ones are the notes that link to those notes, so with one degree of separation from Theory of Mind.)

Its efficiency, saved effort and organisation all in one. 
There are a multitude of other ways to sort and organise files beyond just links, like tags, folders, a variety of file properties, etc, and theyre all incredibly valuable. 

Decentralised, individualistic thinking; its changed my life. 

**Strawberry** 
Strawberry is my choice of music player, given my [[The Joys of Local Music|local collection]], and is lovely. Its a very friendly interface, lots of customisation and management options and is just all-round well made. 
As far as I know its free on linux but paid on Windows, due to upkeeping windows code being more of a pain for the developer. 
Foobar seems like the main local player I see but I never got on with it, never felt right to me.

**Krita**
I'm a CSP refugee (it being the only piece of software I actually lost moving to Linux), and krita has been a nice rebound for me. Its got its quirks for sure but is also plenty functional, and I've dug up a nice selection of community brushes that I'm really fond of, particularly [TOMB of NULLs Stipple Beast series.](https://tombofnull.itch.io/stipple-beast-evolved-brush-pack)

**yt-dlp** 
[yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) is hands down the best media ripper that I know of. 
It can do hundreds of websites, dozens of file types, a bunch of different encoding methods and a library of specific options and arguments. 
If you're at all comfortable with CLI tools, use yt-dlp. Beyond useful tool. 

**Syncthing**
A wonderful P2P syncing program, its what allows me to take my Obsidian vault across multiple devices. Free and open source and really straightforward, I havent found much better syncing solutions for my usecases. 

**OBS**
Self explanatory: robust, flexible, community driven, theres a reason several industries are build off the back of this thing.  
The posterchild of open source.
