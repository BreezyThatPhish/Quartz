*This is an informal run down I have used from a previous explanation for the meantime, this will likely be revamped*

lapajensi is all an exploration of the condition of consciousness, limited perspective, and meaning the idea that your entire existence is your own singular experience of it, and so functionally speaking for you reality really starts and ends at your own experience with the world, thus whatever you experience may well be reality. 
If something has a certain meaning to you, it has that meaning, if you believe a certain thing (that isnt otherwise disproven by experience), that is reality; seasonal example, if you went your entire life believing that santa was real and never had an experience to contradict that, you believed that every night on christmas eve he delivered presents, then functionally _to you_ santa _would_ exist, there is no difference between him being real or not from that POV. 

I find the human experience of meaning to be extremely interesting, because we experience the world in such a strange way because of this aspect of perspective we define _everything_, everything we see has some sort of definition, boundary, rule, meaning, etc. 
You know that trope of robots or computers looking at an image and putting little boxes around every object and labelling them like BOOK, TREE, CAR, whatever? thats basically what we do as well, just a lot less overtly, being an unconscious process 
Like if you look at a forest, what do you see? you see the ground, the trees, the plants, the bushes, the wood, the mushrooms, the grass, etc. 
But thats only because you have those concepts defined to you in your mind, what would the world look like if you didnt? it would just look like a bunch of _stuff_, not necessarily separate from each other. Would you see the trees as different from the ground or grass? where would the barrier between wood and mushroom be drawn, if at all? would they just be seen as one thing? 
its all ultimately just matter, the concept of "difference" between them is a product of conscious perception, but thats not to say its any less "real", whats real or not, meaningful or not, is _also_ an abstract concept, doesnt tangibly exist, and there is no universal ruler to measure that stuff against, meaning is whatever you want it to be; your reality is no less real than someone elses, or the "non conscious" reality its all very brain in a jar.

<br>

Lapajensi exists as an exploration of this, and it does so through an art concept called "[[Hyperart Thomasson|hyperart thomasson]]" AKA "[[Hyperart Thomasson]]", the thing that really inspired this in the first place it details structures that were built with purpose and typically maintained with care that serve no function, whether that be due to faulty design choices, or the environment changing around them to render them inert.
Think a stairway that goes nowhere because where it used to go was since removed, or supports for a bridge that are no longer there, a doorway into a building thats no longer around, paths that are blocked off by newly made walls that now go nowhere, windows bricked over so that you cant see through them, etc. 
Due to often being formed by design oversight a lot of the time pure staircases are honestly really funny, a post youd see on r/designfails or something, but i find a _ton_ of artistic merit in them as an exploration of the attribution of meaning to objects a staircase fundamentally doesnt change whether it goes somewhere or not, it is the exact same thing by every measure, yet our perception of it _completely_ changes depending on whether it does or doesnt "do what its supposed to" how interesting is it that the imaginary concept of an objects "purpose" or "function", something it doesnt innately possess and only exists in our own minds, can entirely restructure how something is perceived. The idea of meaning holds such a power of the perception of the world despite being entirely imaginary, it creates its own little reality separate from the nonconscious.

Another core inspiration is the idea of [post structuralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-structuralism) in language, that basically says that language is not objective and has a two way relationship with the user. For example if you try to find the definition of a word, its described to you with.. more words. At no point does language reach out and directly touch the things that it is creating labels for, instead it exists as a layer above the world, blanketing over it, but being entirely distinct words are also made up by people, and people have opinions on things, and so every word carries with it connotations, implications, adjacent meanings, etc. Not only that but the main intended meanings of the words themselves are subjectively defined by people. 
Yet we perceive reality _through_ language, _through_ words, and so too is our perception of reality shaped by the subjectivity language is ladened with we are as much shaped by words as words are shaped by us, to believe that words describe an "objective" reality, it must be believed that words are entirely accurate (and thus wouldnt need to change), and the wielder of said words is unaffected by them knowledge that you hold is knowledge created _by_ language that is being fed _back through_ itself, its recursive and self referential
All of this is obviously somewhat exaggerated for artistic exploration, but still the concepts hold merit and we find them incredibly valuable and interesting

<br>

Lapajensi is an endlessly large environment made of pure staircases in an attempt to create a "post structuralist environment", designed and purposed structures that were made and placed without said purpose, without meaning.
Named objects now exist without their descriptors, without their words to taint and shape their interpretation, a staircase doesnt exist as a staircase does as it is made without the purpose and function of one, and does not carry with it the concept of a staircase to shape how it is seen, it just is as the stone that makes it up, nothing more its all about taking these things that are otherwise specifically _for_ a function, and taking that function away from them, to exist purely as the matter that makes them up with no sense of meaning attached, and an exploration into what that does for a conscious experience of it 
Anyone who would exist in this place wouldnt have any prior _anything_, they would pop into existence as a blank slate, so that their perspective on the world is entirely shaped by their experience there. 
And so the question would be how would they experience it? would they attempt to define and draw lines in the sand around what they personally judge as various objects, or would they view the thing holistically? as one big singular thing? (should the concept of a "thing" even exist) its a place where the subjective human experience of meaning exists _beneath_ an environment that has no consideration or care for it, to act as a blank canvas to explore how perception shapes a persons reality, how we see purpose in things, how does that change our experience of the world, etc a sensory deprivation chamber for meaning.
