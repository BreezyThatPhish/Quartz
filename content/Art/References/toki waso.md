[[toki pona]] is the language I chose for [[aalto]] due to its personal aversion to specificity and condensation. Its simple and soulful nature helps it be somewhat comfortable communicating through it. 
However it needed some modifications, both due to its lack of a mouth and its lack of hands, so below is my personal sign language system for it. 

toki luka is the hand sign language that anyone can use and toki waso is the feathered sign language built for [[aalto]].

*I have not touched this in a long time and honestly almost forgot about it, so it's rough, but an important part of [[aalto|aalto's]] character all the same*

# Grammar | lawa

## Sentence Structure 

### Word Distinction 

Due to toki luka being a spelling sign language, its important to know where one word ends and another begins.
Words can be separated from one another in two ways: manual signing, or a swish of the tail.  

The former is signed by clenching both fists simultaneously; proper form has them held vertically parallel to one another, however for comfort this typically isnt strictly adhered to.  

The latter is what gets considerably more use from [[aalto]], as its far more comfortable. The tail is flicked from one side of the body to the other, for utmost clarity this typically moves in a vertical arc, but any movement that brings the tip from one side to the other is considered valid. Similarly, clear signage waits for the tail to complete its movement before beginning the new word, however with high fluency the beginning of the movement can be enough, allowing for a continuous stream of signs with no pause. 

### Sentence Distinction

Sentences are distinguished from one another by a noticeable pause in signage, there are no gestures or signs for it. 

### Sentence Blocks 

[[toki pona]] sentences have four main sections that mark what they are referring to: the subject, predicate, object and preposition. 

In toki waso, these sections are marked by specific gestures of [[aalto|aaltos]] head wings, with what is said under tehse positions being attributed to being under the respective sentence section, eg what is said under the "subject" position is attributed to being (and/or being in reference to) the subject.
The movement between these positions function as the words that would otherwise signify transition between these sections, removing the need to manually sign them with spell-signing, such as how moving from the subject to the predicate position says "li" (marks the predicate), and moving from the predicate to object position says "e" (marks the object). 

As prepositions can be marked by a variety of prepositional words, anything said under the preposition sign is attributed to being a preposition phrase, rather than the wing movbements signifying any specific transition word. As such, prepositional words like "lon" or "tan" are still spell-signed for necessary specificity.

#### la

"la" marks the phrase before it as something that the rest of the sentence and / or phrase is in reference to / in the context of. So to say "kala la telo li pona" means "in the context of fish (kala), water (telo) is good (pona)", which more naturally is to say "fish like water".

"la" phrases require a bit more work to signify, as they can contain any of the above sentence blocks within them due to being able to function as full sentences, and so must be indicated somewhat independently.  
They're marked by the head wings being held vertically, with the lower wing being pointed either upwards or out as a distinguisher, echoing the non-la positions.

### Wing Modifiers 

toki waso takes advantage of [[aalto|aalto's]] back right wing by having them function as modifiers, centring around questions, "a!" and "o!". 
As the left wing is used for spell-signing only the right is used for modifiers, and as such they operate entirely independent of one another. 

#### Questions 

[[toki pona]] has a variety of ways to phrase questions, and as such so does toki waso. A question state is indicated by a partly opened wing, as pictured below. This is referred to as the "question flag" position. 
![[Pasted image 20260927133836.png]]
Whilst the phrasing of questions differs from [[toki pona]], the methods of answering them does not. 

#### anu seme

In [[toki pona]], appending 'anu seme' to the end of a sentence turns the sentence into a yes or no question. 
However in toki waso, the question flag may be held for the sentences duration to mark it as an "anu seme" question, functionally appending any sentence that happens under it with "anu seme". 

<br>

#### ijo ala ijo 
Another way to phrase yes or no questions is using the "ijo ala ijo" structure, wherein the thing you are questioning is duplicated and placed either side of "ala" to indicate a yes or no relationship. For example, "suwi ala suwi" may appear as a way of asking if the subject in question is cute/sweet or not. 
Whilst ala exists as a word in its own right, in the context of questions it is signified only by a double flap of the question flag, moving as: closed, open, closed, open (pictured below), thus it is not spell-signed when used for questions.  
![[Pasted image 20250426180059.png|100]]![[Pasted image 20250426175504.png|135]]![[Pasted image 20250426180059.png|100]]![[Pasted image 20250426175504.png|135]]
This is done during the signing of the word that is being questioned, and marks that word with a "ijo ala ijo" structure. 
So to say "suwi ala suwi", the above wing movement would be gestured whilst "suwi" is being spelt, noteably without the need to repeat "suwi". 
The question flag position is still maintained for the rest of the sentence, both before and after the "ijo ala ijo" question, so as to make it clear its a question phrase, but this allows for more specificity as to what is being questioned. 

<br> 

##### seme
To ask open ended questions, "seme" is used on its own and replaces the word you are asking about. 
For example, you could say "**seme** li lon supa" to ask "what is on the table", and the answer could be "**mani mi** li lon supa" (my money is on the table).  "seme" occupies the space that the answer does as a way to prompt someone to fill it in with the relevant information. 

Similarly to "ala", seme is replaced by a wing gesture, a single flap between closed and open (pictured below), and as with "ala" the question flag is still maintained for the rest of the sentence (both before and after) to properly mark it as a question phrase. 
![[Pasted image 20250426180059.png|100]]![[Pasted image 20250426175504.png|135]]
"seme" is often still signed out to fill space, with this wing flap happening during its spelling, as there is no word to necessarily apply it to like "ala" due to "seme" already functioning as a stand-in word, however this is not *entirely* necessary. 
"seme" *can* be gestured during a pause in signage and mean the same thing, however this is typically not done as it can be confused with sentence separation; to sign *and* gesture "seme" together is simply clearer.  

<br>

#### a
"a" is signified by a full extension of the wings that maintain their open-ness for the remaining duration of the "a" phrase. This means that unlike questions, "a" phrases are flagged only from when the "a" appears in the sentence. 
So whilst "a sina lape" would be flagged at the beginning and maintained for the entire phrase, "sina lape a" would only be flagged at the end.  
![[Pasted image 20250427230731.png|185]]

If a phrase is both a question and modified by "a" before the question occurs things are more complicated. 
The "a" flag takes priority over the question flag when relevant, such as being maintained during non-question parts of a question phrase, and question grammar takes on more traditional placement; that is to say, the question flag is only signed exactly where the question elements would go. 
For "ala" and "seme" questions, this is no problem, as they already have unique flaps with specific placements. "anu seme" questions however are slightly modified, with the question flag being transitioned to at the end of a sentence much as you would place "anu seme" at the end of a sentence in traditional [[toki pona]]. The "a" flag is still returned to after this before starting the next phrase. 
The transition between "a" and question flags is unique, with the wing moving directly between the two with no full retraction, as pictured below.

#### o
"o" is marked by a full wing extension into a full retraction, unlike "a" it is not maintained. It is flagged exactly when "o" occurs in the phrase, not before or after it appears. 
![[Pasted image 20250427230731.png|185]]![[Pasted image 20250426180059.png|100]]
This makes it simpler to implement into multi-modified sentences, as it simply occurs as a gesture before returning to whatever "default" state the phrase has, whether that be an "a" or question flag. 
However, it is defined by unique transitions between these two other modifiers. 
To transition between "a" and "o", fully retract, then fully extend, then fully retract to mark "o", then fully extend again to return to "a" flag. 
To transition between question and "o", fully retract, then fully extend, then fully retract to mark "o", then partially extend again to return to question flag. 
This is distinct from transitions between "a" and question flags, as those have no retraction and instead move smoothly between the two.
