[toki pona](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/Main_Page) is a [conlang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructed_language) designed around minimalism. With ~120 words, it is highly abstract and context dependent. It is what you've been seeing me use around the site.

There are a variety of benefits and appeals for this system, but the one I find most compelling, and the reason I choose to speak it, is that it serves as a form of meditation. 
I have a very complicated relationship with communication, often times struggling against the fact that to communicate is to change, to compress and to reshape; you must create a shippable package of meaning to be sent over to another party, who then unpacks it however they wish. Thoughts cannot transmit as they are, they must be fit into existing frameworks of understanding with their own pre-existing interpretations. For this reason, I can find vulnerable communication difficult, as none of what I wish to share with someone can truly ever be shared due to the impact transporation has.
And so, I'm drawn to toki pona on the basis of its simplicity, as it is far more soulful and focused on taking the world as it is, rather than fitting the world into what we think it is. 

Word meanings are broad and vague, and are generally understood to be imprecise, intentionally levering the more internal and unspoken understandings people have of the world to communicate. 
It is impossible to reconstruct the feeling of grass on your palms, sun on your skin or tears in your eyes with words, and so toki pona does not seek to do so, instead understanding that you will work off of your own experience of these things. 

Furthermore, the way we speak has a complicated but significant relationship with the way we think, and so by using language we reinforce the perspectives and worldviews that those language structures are built off of. 
So, by using language that is far simpler and less projective, so too do you lessen that part of your worldview, at least in part. A forest no longer becomes a collection of trees, leaves, soil, ground, detritus etc, its "land", a relationship with someone close is no longer that of a lover or a friend or a sibling it is "a strong bond". 

This simple and introspective nature has greatly endeared me to the language, and is why I choose to write the most sensitive parts of myself through it. 
It is not a difficult language to learn, and is a fantastic way to broaden your perspective beyond more traditional societal outlooks.
