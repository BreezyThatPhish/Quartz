[Hyperart Thomasson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperart_Thomasson) is a concept introduced by Japanese artist [Genpei Akasegawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genpei_Akasegawa) that describes things that were built for function but, due to a variety of possible reasons, no longer fulfill said function and instead sit useless. 
The most prominent example of hyperart is that of the "pure staircase", a staircase that only goes up or down without any destination, nothing at the top / bottom. 
Hyperart can be caused by a shifting environment rendering an existing structure useless, faulty design choices at a structures inception, neglect, amidst other reasons.

Rather insultingly, the "Thomasson" part of the namesake comes from a professional baseball player, [Gary Thomasson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Thomasson). He was a strong player for years, playing for a variety of US teams, until he was signed on for a Japanese team, the [Yomiuri Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Giants).
The price of his signing to the [Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Giants) was a new record for the [Nippon League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball), but despite this his performance was dismal. He spent the majority of his incredibly high paying contract, benched, and out of use. A useless piece of architecture, paid for and preserved, to do absolutely nothing. 
This is why [Akasegawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genpei_Akasegawa) chose to name the phenomenon after him; apt, if insulting. 

I was introduced to the concept via msx's [Pure Staircase Album](https://lapfox.bandcamp.com/album/pure-staircase), a wonderful noise album, and since then it has been the focus of a great and prolonged fascination. 

<br>

## Artistic Merit 

I find thomassons rich with the potential for commentary on the human condition and conscious perception.
What separates a functional and non-functional structure is entirely immaterial. Sure if a staircases destination vanishes or is blocked off thats a physical change, but the staircase is still a staircase, it still allows one to ascend and descend its steps, it still carries someoen to a different point in space. The steps are no less steps whether they have a destination or not, yet despite this our perception of them entirely changes based on this fact. 
A staircase that goes nowhere is useless, strange and often times comical, whereas one that goes somewhere is benign, expected, typical. 

We project our ideas and expectations for the world so strongly onto it that it utterly changes our experience of it. Things play by rules, there is function to things, there is right and wrong, normal and strange, all treated as if they're innate qualities to the world itself, when in reality they are entirely make believe. 

This is a very large and broad concept that can be applied to the conscious experience in general, but I find hyperart an incredibly compelling microcosm of it. It reveals so much about how we experience the world in the most humble way, most often being overlooked or forgotten. 

<br>

## Foraging for Art in Urban Spaces
I use the term "urban" here loosely as really any human-designed space is liable to produce hyperart, however urban spaces do so with the most frequency and quantity due to their strictly designed and often highly volatile nature.
Regardless, hyperart is something that you can find in your own daily life if you look for it, something that is as unique as it is enriching. 

I got into [[Photography|photography]] explicitly for hyperart, I wanted to go looking around and photographing what I could find, and I found loads. 
It made me explore painfully familiar places with new eyes and new enthusiasm, I noticed the little things even beyond hyperart, and whatever I found always revealed something so human about places that can often struggle to feel that way; like ruts in the dirt where those who came before pushed off from to realign themselves.

To explore an area you may have passed through a hundred times already for something new is an incredibly unique experience, to even hunt for art in the first place is unique, let alone something so unintentional and symptomatic. 
Its an incredibly special joy to me, and brings a level of excitement to every trip outdoors I take, much less going somewhere new.
