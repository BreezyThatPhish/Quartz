#### <font style="color:grey"> telo kasi mi tawa kon </font>
Tea has quickly become one of my most passionate interests, catalysed by my time abroad in Malaysia. It was to the point I brought home a second suitcase, of which around 2/3rds of the contents were tea related. I have a whole cabinet in my room dedicated entirely to tea, not helped by its interaction with another obsession of mine: mugs and pottery. 
![[P1050069.jpg]]
There are several ways to enjoy tea: a typical and simple cup, a large and prolonged pot, loose leaf brewing with care put towards the steeping time and methods, engaging with the whole ceremony of [gong fu cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongfu_tea), so on. The variety in both the methods and the teas themselves make it a vast and deep hobby, filled with history and spanning continents. 
Also, for those wondering, the little clay fox is known as a [tea pet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_pet). Mostly just a good luck charm to have whilst you drink, but they do have some direct involvements with the tea drinking process, namely to have excess water poured over them as "feeding", or as a temperature indicator for the water (watching how it evaporates). 

Tea as a drink is incredibly versatile, there is a monumental breadth of tastes it covers. Dark and mulled spiced blends, nutty and aromatic black teas, earthy and dry green teas, flowery and bitter jasmines.
Im lucky enough to have a dedicated tea shop in a nearby town, floor to ceiling shelves carrying a rather extensive collection. Equally, given my variety and willingness to resteep, any given bag lasts for a good period of time. A few months typically. 
Im primarily a green and white tea person, though I appreciate something from nearly every type Ive tried. Some of my favourites include gabalong, jasmine, hojicha (toasted green tea), gong mei white, sencha (fog island sencha is gorgeous) and matcha. Matcha is one of my utmost home comforts at this point, I love it as plain as it can come. Frothed milk and a heap of powder creates this porous and pillowy drink that tastes like lawnmower clippings. 

However the more fulfilling part of the hobby to me is the meditative action of it. [Gong fu cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongfu_tea) is a methodology that emphasises brewing and drinking smaller portions of tea at a time, the idea being that you get a stronger water to leaf ratio.
There are many reasons for this, partly it helps to bring out the taste better, but equally it asks you to pay more attention *to* the taste. You brew the same leaves several times, drink in little shots, and so taste how the leaves change between each steep.
This process forces you to be slow and deliberate, paying greater attention to the whole ordeal. It becomes a mindful and meditative exercise, something soulful and gentle. 
![[P1050224.jpg]]
Whenever Im stressed, whenever Im feeling tired, whenever Im looking to decompress, I get out my little teaset and settle down with a steep of something from my cabinet, spending the next while cupping warm pottery and rolling flavours & smells over.
Its a gentle and grounding experience, one I appreciate more everytime I do it.
