Plants have been a hobby that kind of exploded into my life over the past few years. Im lucky enough to have a space largely to myself at home, a detached garage that we converted the loft of to be like an apartment (though without any water), and as soon as I had that kind of control over a space I began filling it with plants without really thinking about it. 
They began as an obvious pick to decorate the space, but instead of stopping at two or three, I just kept seeing and finding ones that I liked. And as the quantity in my room grew, so did my earnest passion for them, almost lagging behind the number I actually owned for a while.

Plants bring several boons into my life and space, from aesthetics to air quality to mental soothing. Their benefits are extensively documented, but frankly I had felt them before I ever actually learnt about them. 
Beyond those though, the most significant love I have for them is the nurturing relationship they bring to my routine. 

I feel such a genuine and vulnerable warmth to every little shoot and every weathered leaf in my room. If something falters, I have an urgency to help. If something grows, I feel that gentle glowing sense of pride. When I look at a plant that has been with me for some extended period of time, or has some memorable characteristic or story to it, I feel the comfort of a familiar face. 
It's not exaggeration to say that I feel the plants in my room are as my peers, regular and individualist presences in my day to day life that I share a reciprocal relationship with. 

My fiddle leaf fig for example, one of the first plants I ever owned. Fussy things I'm told, though mine has been rather content in my time caring for it. But additionally to being one of the first ones to enter my room, it was one of the first ones that I had to learn how to save. 
I, as many new plant owners do, overwatered the thing and it began suffering from root rot. It was a problem I took the time to research, diagnose and fix in my own time, because I cared for it. 
I went out and bought new soil, hauled it outside, stripped it out of its pot and soil, chopped off any unhealthy roots and repotted it with the new soil, as well is mixing in some amount of gravel to help with drainage. 
It lost a couple leaves to the rot but it did survive, and grew two new ones over the course of a couple weeks after the incident. And it still is sitting in its original spot, happy and stable. 
I think about that little arc everytime I see it, and Im reminded for the love I have for it. 

For another anecdote, my largest plant as of writing is my first monstera deliciosa, which was a plant I took from my mum. It was pretty sad with her, droopy and constantly sodden wet, she handed it over to me mostly on the basis that she didnt know how to properly care for it. 
I didnt know any better in the moment myself, but through putting in the time to, again, repot it with new soil, re-sit and tie up some better supports for its heavy stems, and placing it in the best lighting spot I could find, it has been maybe my most actively growing plant just behind my second smaller monstera. 
It is constantly growing new shoots and new leaves, and recently has a whole new one coming right up out of the soil. The beginning of a new main branch I hope. 

Most of my plants have little chronologies to them like this, and every single one of them have the potential to create them.
Those stories illustrate that reciprocal relationship between plant and carer. You put in time and effort to see them happy and healthy, and they show it in the most blatant and beautiful ways.
The love and nurture I put in is reflected back at me in the form of new leaves, shifting shapes and healthy colours. Its fantastically direct and overt, which is a large part of why plantcare is so rewarding, because everytime I walk into my room I see the fruits of my love and labour.

I strongly encourage anyone who can spare the small amount of effort and money it takes to maintain a plant to do so. Even the simplest little thing can become a nurtured connection.
Succulents, snake plants, philodendrons, monsteras, ivys, anthuriums, cacti, theres an impossible large range out there to choose from, surely one that works for you. There really is nothing comparable to a room full of life.
